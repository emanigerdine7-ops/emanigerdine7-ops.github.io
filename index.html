    The "Benito Bowl" Was a Statement, Not a Translation

    February 9, 2026

    Last night’s Super Bowl LX halftime show was a masterpiece of representation. While the hate on social media focuses on the language barrier, they are missing the point. Bad Bunny did exactly what Kendrick Lamar did last year—he used the world’s biggest stage to represent his people with zero apologies.

    The most emotional moment was the tribute to Liam Conejo Ramos. By handing his actual Grammy to a young boy on stage (representing the 5-year-old recently detained by ICE), Benito made a powerful political statement that didn't need a single word of English to be understood.

    The show was about the vibe, the energy, and the culture. From the domino players to the salsa remixes, it was a party for everyone. If you couldn't understand the lyrics, there were captions, but the real goal was to dance and vibe. Music is universal, and Benito proved you don't need to speak English to command the room.

    The hate isn't deserved—it was a great show for people of Spanish descent and for all of us.
